3. EVIDENCE SCHEMAS AND FLOW
Existing source callbacks -> immutable observation -> existing off-path proof worker -> Archive codec -> existing CompressedJournal. The separate information process uses its existing DurablePublisher worker and its own explicitly isolated archive path. No new upstream calls are required. All serialization, hashing, replay, compression, fsync and archive disk work occur off the trading decision path. Source admission does not wait for queue capacity or archive completion.

Envelope: schema BTC15_OBSERVATION_EVIDENCE_V1; evidence_class FIXTURE or NATIVE_CAPTURE; session; sequence; previous_sha256; kind; payload; sha256; signal_only=true; orders=false; production_enabled=false; actionable=false. Hash/sequence chains are session-specific. START declares a continuity boundary; STOP records orderly closure. Restart does not silently bridge old history.

SOURCE stores value, original source/receipt clocks, feed, epoch, availability time and content-derived publication/observation identity. History has no contract ownership and is retained across rollover. BTC's capture-process epoch is explicitly distinguished from an unavailable upstream epoch. BRTI retains its Delivery owner epoch. SOURCE_REPEAT preserves later observation receipts by reference while keeping the publication's first receipt. Recovery stores first recovery availability with original_receipt_ts=null, and cannot become original evidence. Source/conflict/clock/gap/restart events are explicit. Native joins require the exact original decision cutoff; a later BRTI update cannot be attached to an earlier decision.

QUOTE_BYTES preserves exact already-received websocket message bytes. QUOTE_RECEIVE stores application ws.recv-return time and monotonic clock; kernel/wire receipt remains unknown. QUOTE_ACCEPT records after the unchanged decoder accepts the same bytes. Market/stream IDs, epochs, sequence numbers, prices and quantities remain in the message. Offline same-side bid/ask reconstruction reports sequence gaps, reconnects and missing snapshots. Consumer timestamps are never relabeled as receipts. Displayed depth never establishes fills or queue priority.

QUOTE_PROOF links the full native witness to the original proof SHA256. Event JSON member order is retained for byte-exact reconstruction. Receive validation checks every proof event, including its quantities, rather than accepting a sequence identity alone. A changed-depth proof with the same identity fails that association.

INFORMATION_INPUT retains a compact skeleton, raw-input hash, decision and information-capture cutoffs, publication outcome and frame ID where an exact raw match exists. Overlapping ticks, bars and quote arrays use bounded trim/append VECTOR records. Raw buffers are not repeated at each publication. Reconstruction must reproduce the original canonical input bytes and proof byte hashes. Input-copy history remains distinguishable from original source events. Failed and malformed input attempts produce explicit evidence failures.

HTTP_BYTES/HTTP_RESPONSE retain whitelisted official market/series response bodies, original local response-decode receipt and hash. Official ticker, open/close, floor strike and rules or observed series fee fields are retained. Unsupported fee formula, rounding, effective period and scope stay explicitly missing. Account/portfolio/order endpoints and credential headers are excluded. The offline ledger retains setup, confirmation, ticket, cancellation and expiry identities, including unmatched cases. FINAL/management/EXIT joins require original contract/window/target/side/origin, original event time and detached archive references. It does not authenticate current archives, calculate opportunity labels, infer manual actions or establish fee/fill evidence.

Existing proof admission charges its 2 MiB ceiling. Large messages are rejected with a gap, never silently truncated.

Per archive: vector <=8192 rows and <=3 MiB serialized; retained vector-object graph <=32 MiB; input <=3 MiB; record <=5 MiB raw; gzip segment <=8 MiB; archive <=256 MiB. Limits may only be lowered. No age retention, pruning, overwriting of prior segments or production retention change. Quota exhaustion stops capture and marks incomplete evidence. Separate source and information archive instances have separate limits; two default archives imply at most 512 MiB of archive data, not a shared 256 MiB pool. None was created in production.

Incremental resident-memory planning gate: 128 MiB per active archive instance, pending live qualification. Queue/cache/record guards are enforced; this is not an OS RSS limit. The small sizing fixture traced less than 1 MiB of Python allocations and recorded no drops; it is not a maximum-load/RSS certificate. It measured approximately 557 compressed bytes per unique source observation and 1,588 bytes per quote message including receive/accept/hash overhead.

Illustrative source/quote-only rates, assuming one BTC and one BRTI observation/second:
1 quote message/second: about 223 MiB/day; 256 MiB reached in 27.6 hours.
10 messages/second: about 1,401 MiB/day; quota reached in 4.4 hours.
100 messages/second: about 13,179 MiB/day; quota reached in 0.47 hours.
These are synthetic small-message estimates and exclude information/proof/metadata additions. They are not measured current feed rates. A sustained installation needs separately approved storage/export sizing.

A worker stall leaves a bounded queue and drops new evidence; source/strategy results do not wait. Idle flushing records overflow counts. An archive error disables further writes in that instance, emits a diagnostic/status and leaves an incomplete session. A full or failed disk cannot guarantee a durable marker on itself. The missing clean STOP, in-memory status and later restart boundary expose that limitation. New sessions never append behind torn data. The reader reports corrupt segments and sequence/hash failures and does not silently claim complete coverage. Existing segments are never repaired or deleted. Offline reading is also byte-bounded.
